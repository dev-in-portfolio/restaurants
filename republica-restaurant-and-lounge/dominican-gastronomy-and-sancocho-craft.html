The Hallmarks of Dominican Gastronomy

Every dish begins with raw ingredients, fresh tropical produce, and patient slow-cooking.

Foundation 01

The Aromatic Sofrito Base

Every authentic Dominican stew begins with freshly minced culantro (recao), whole roasted garlic, sweet ajíes gustosos peppers, white onions, and wild mountain Dominican oregano sautéed in annatto oil until fragrant and deeply savory.

Foundation 02

The Sancocho Ritual

More than a soup, Sancocho is the Dominican national soul stew prepared for family reunions, rainfall days, and celebratory milestones. Simmered in cast aluminum calderos with bone-in beef shank, corn, yuca, ñame, and green plantains until rich and velvety.

Foundation 03

The Twice-Fried Tostone

Green plantains sliced thick, gently blanched in hot oil, hand-smashed flat in a wooden tostonera, and flash-fried a second time to achieve an airy, crispy exterior surrounding a tender, savory interior.

The Flag of Flavor

La Bandera: The Culinary Symbol of Home

Named after the red, white, and blue of the Dominican flag, La Bandera represents the comfort of every Dominican home. The white fluffy rice represents purity, the rich red stewed kidney beans represent passion, and the succulent braised meat represents national strength—served with sweet fried maduros or crispy salty tostones.
